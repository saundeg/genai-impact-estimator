Generative AI Environmental Impact Estimator

Estimates the annual inference electricity, carbon and water associated with generative AI provided to staff and students under an institutional licence. Every input below is colour-coded by how well it is evidenced, because the reliability of the answer is set almost entirely by the weakest input — not the strongest.

01 Who has access

Split staff and students. They adopt AI at different rates and use it for different things, and averaging them hides most of the variation.

02 How much they use it

Pick a starting scenario, then adjust. Note that adoption is now evidenced — what is genuinely uncertain is how much of that use runs through your licences rather than personal free accounts.

Students

HEPI 2026: 95% of UK undergraduates report using AI in at least one way.

The single biggest uncertainty in this model. HEPI cannot tell paid institutional ChatGPT from free personal accounts.

No published UK figure exists for prompt frequency.

Staff

Weak evidence. US working-age adoption for work was 36–45% in 2024–25; information and technology sectors reached 56–76%.

Usually higher than students: staff have institutional accounts and data-handling rules.

Adjust upward if Copilot or coding assistants are widely deployed.

Short text query Published

Oviedo et al., Joule (2026): median 0.31 Wh, interquartile range 0.16–0.60, for a frontier-scale model (>200B parameters) at production scale. Cross-checks against Google's metered median Gemini prompt of 0.24 Wh.

Long reasoning query Published

Oviedo et al.: at roughly 5,000 output tokens (15× a typical query), the median rises 13× to 3.91 Wh, interquartile range 2.15–7.05.

Uncertainty ranges are recomputed from these fields and from the task mix. If you override a central value, the range moves with it — the reported range will always bracket the reported central estimate.

04 Carbon and water conversion

Electricity is only the intermediate step. These factors turn kilowatt-hours into the two things a sustainability report actually needs.

Carbon

Grid emissions factor, kgCO₂e/kWh Published

DESNZ 2026 UK electricity generation factor: 0.13096, down from 0.17700 in 2025. This is the location-based factor — the average carbon content of grid electricity. Add transmission and distribution losses if your reporting framework requires them.

Supplier market-based cross-check, gCO₂e/prompt Measured

Google reports 0.03 gCO₂e for the median Gemini text prompt — 0.023 g Scope 2 market-based plus 0.010 g Scope 1 and 3. Market-based accounting credits the supplier's renewable energy purchases, so it reads far lower than the location-based figure. Applies to standard text prompts only.

Water

Direct cooling water, L/kWh Measured

Water Usage Effectiveness, ISO/IEC 30134-9. Published fleet figures: AWS 0.12 (2025), Microsoft 0.27 (FY2025), industry average about 0.36 per Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Google roughly 1.1 implied by its 0.26 mL per 0.24 Wh prompt. Microsoft's per-query range starts at 0.0 because some sites are closed-loop or air-cooled.

Indirect water from power generation, L/kWh Published

Water consumed upstream generating the electricity. Often 80% or more of the true total. Published values: about 1.8 for a generic European grid, 3.92 disclosed by Meta for purchased electricity, 7.6 estimated by Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Set to zero if you want direct cooling water only.

Read the small result carefully. This model will almost certainly tell you that AI inference is a negligible share of your footprint. That is a true statement about inference within this boundary, and not a finding that AI is environmentally insignificant. Excluded from the boundary: training the models, manufacturing the hardware, constructing and operating the wider data-centre estate, and the electricity your users' own laptops draw. Embedded supply-chain water alone exceeds 99% of the corporate water footprint at one major operator. Do not let this tool be quoted as an all-in figure.

What that means in everyday terms

Raw kilowatt-hours mean little to most readers. These comparisons put the figures on a human scale. The basis for each is shown so you can check it — or swap it for one you prefer.

    Handle with care. Equivalences are illustrative, never evidence. The denominator you pick controls the impression: the same figure can be made to sound trivial or alarming depending on whether you compare it to boiling a kettle or to powering homes. Quote the basis alongside the comparison, and never substitute an equivalence for the actual number.

    Suggested wording for a committee paper. “Illustrative scenario modelling of institutionally licensed generative AI inference, pending supplier usage data. Adoption rates are drawn from published survey evidence; interaction frequency and licence share are planning assumptions and are the dominant source of uncertainty.”
